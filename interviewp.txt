 AI Interview Notes — Tasks 1–9
 1. Generative AI
Q: What is Generative AI?
A: Generative AI refers to models that generate new content such as text, code, images, audio, or video based on learned patterns.
 2. LLM
Q: What is an LLM?
A: A Large Language Model is a neural network trained on large amounts of text to understand and generate natural language.
Q: How does an LLM generate text?
A: It predicts the next token based on the tokens and context provided to it.
 3. Tokens
Q: What is a token?
A: A token is a unit of text processed by an LLM. It may be a word, part of a word, punctuation, or another text fragment.
Q: Why are tokens important?
A: Token count affects context-window limits, latency and API cost.
Q: Does one token equal one word?
A: No. A word can consist of multiple tokens, and a token can represent only part of a word.
 4. Temperature
Q: What is temperature?
A: Temperature controls the randomness of model output. Lower values generally produce more predictable responses; higher values produce more varied responses.
Q: What temperature would you use for RAG?
A: Usually a low temperature because factual, grounded and consistent responses are preferred.
Q: Does temperature = 0 guarantee deterministic output?
A: No. It can improve reproducibility but does not guarantee identical output because other model, infrastructure and provider factors can introduce variation.
 5. Embeddings
Q: What is an embedding?
A: An embedding is a numerical vector representation of data such as text that captures semantic characteristics.
Q: Why do we need embeddings?
A: They allow us to represent text mathematically so that semantically similar content can be found using vector similarity.
Q: What is the difference between text, tokens, embeddings and vectors?
A:

An embedding is the representation; the vector is the numerical representation produced by the embedding model.
 6. Vector Similarity
Q: What is vector similarity?
A: It measures how similar two vectors are, allowing us to identify semantically related content.
Q: What is cosine similarity?
A: It measures the angle between two vectors. Smaller angular difference generally means greater similarity.
Q: What is Euclidean distance?
A: It measures the straight-line distance between two vectors.
Q: Cosine similarity vs Euclidean distance?
* Cosine → compares direction.
* Euclidean → compares absolute distance.
* The appropriate metric depends on the embedding model and vector database configuration.
Q: Does semantic search always use cosine similarity?
A: No. Semantic search uses embeddings plus a vector similarity/distance function. Depending on the vector database and configuration, this may be cosine similarity, dot product, Euclidean distance, or another metric.
Q: What does Qdrant use?
A: Qdrant supports multiple distance metrics, including cosine, dot product and Euclidean. The collection's configuration determines which one is used.
 7. Vector Database
Q: What is a vector database?
A: A database optimized for storing vectors and performing similarity search, often with metadata filtering.
Q: Why use Qdrant for RAG?
A: Qdrant provides vector storage, similarity search and metadata filtering, making it suitable for semantic retrieval in RAG applications.

 9. RAG
Q: What is RAG?
A: Retrieval-Augmented Generation combines information retrieval with LLM generation. Relevant external information is retrieved and supplied to the LLM as context.
Q: Why use RAG?
A: To provide current, private or domain-specific information without requiring the model itself to contain that information in its parameters.
 10. RAG vs Fine-Tuning
Q: RAG vs fine-tuning?
RAG:
* Adds external knowledge at query time.
* Knowledge can be updated without retraining.
* Good for enterprise documents and frequently changing information.
* Provides source/citation opportunities.
Fine-tuning:
* Changes model behavior/weights through additional training.
* Useful for style, format, behavior or domain-specific patterns.
* Updating factual knowledge through fine-tuning is usually less convenient than updating a RAG knowledge base.
Interview answer:
> "I would generally use RAG for changing or private enterprise knowledge and fine-tuning when I need to change model behavior, style or task-specific capabilities."

 13. Chunking
Q: What is chunking?
A: Splitting large documents into smaller pieces so they can be embedded, retrieved and supplied to the LLM efficiently.
Q: How do you choose chunk size?
A: Based on document structure, semantic coherence, retrieval quality, context limits and evaluation results. There is no universal optimal size.
Q: Fixed-size vs semantic chunking?
* Fixed-size → simple and predictable.
* Semantic → tries to preserve meaningful boundaries such as paragraphs, sections or topics.
Q: What is chunk overlap?
A: The amount of content shared between adjacent chunks to avoid losing context at chunk boundaries.
Q: What if chunks are too small?
A: Context can be fragmented, retrieval may require many chunks, and important relationships can be lost.
Q: What if chunks are too large?
A: Retrieval becomes less precise, irrelevant information increases, and more tokens are sent to the LLM.

 15. Hallucination
Q: What is hallucination?
A: When an LLM generates information that is incorrect, unsupported or fabricated.

      JAR

 Q16. What is a Deployment?

Deployment

 Q17. Why not create Pods manually?
Pods are ephemeral.

A Deployment defines:

 Q18. What is a Kubernetes Service?
A Service provides a stable networking abstraction over a set of Pods.
Pods can be destroyed and recreated, so their IP addresses can change.
The Service provides a stable endpoint:

Service
 Q19. What is a ConfigMap?
A ConfigMap stores non-sensitive configuration.

 Q20. What is a Kubernetes Secret?
A Secret is designed to hold sensitive configuration such as:

credentials
For production AWS deployments, I would prefer integrating Kubernetes with a managed secret store such as AWS Secrets Manager rather than maintaining sensitive values directly in Git-managed manifests.
 Q21. What is HPA?
HPA stands for Horizontal Pod Autoscaler.
It automatically adjusts the number of Pod replicas based on metrics.
Low traffic
2 Pods
High traffic
4 Pods
Very high traffic
8 Pods
It can use CPU/memory and, with the appropriate metrics infrastructure, application-level metrics.
 Q22. Why might CPU alone be insufficient for scaling an AI application?
AI workloads aren't always CPU-bound.
Useful signals can include:
Request rate
Concurrent requests
LLM workload
For an LLM/RAG application, request concurrency or queue depth may sometimes represent demand better than CPU utilization.

 Q24. What is EKS?

 PART 9 — Enterprise RAG Security
 Q32. Where should authorization happen in an enterprise RAG system?

Correct:

Retrieve everything

AND

Only authorized chunks enter the context.
 PART 10 — Hybrid Search
 Q34. Why use hybrid search?
Dense semantic search and keyword search have complementary strengths.
Semantic search handles:
meaning
paraphrases
conceptual similarity
Keyword search handles:
exact terms
IDs
acronyms
product names
Combining them generally improves enterprise retrieval robustness.
 Q35. What is RRF?
RRF means Reciprocal Rank Fusion.
It combines rankings from multiple retrieval systems.
A common formulation is:
[
RRF(d)=\sum_i\frac{1}{k+rank_i(d)}
]
Vector ranking
      +

Combined ranking
 PART 11 — Reranking
 Q36. Why do we rerank?
Initial retrieval should be fast and broad.
Reranking performs a more expensive relevance assessment on a smaller candidate set.
10M chunks
Fast retrieval
10 high-quality chunks
This balances retrieval quality with latency.
 Q37. Why not rerank the entire corpus?
Because reranking is computationally more expensive.
Doing it over millions of documents would be too slow and expensive.
Cheap retrieval
Small candidate set
Expensive reranking
 PART 12 — Context Engineering
 Q38. Why can't we simply put every retrieved document into the prompt?

 Q39. What is context engineering?
Context engineering is the deliberate design of the information provided to the model, including:

* ordering
* prioritization
* token budget
* output requirements
It's broader than simply constructing a prompt.

 PART 15 — Evaluation
 Q45. What should we evaluate in RAG?
At least two major areas:
 Retrieval
 Generation
Faithfulness / groundedness
We should evaluate the retrieval and generation stages separately because a bad answer can originate from either stage.
 Q46. Why separate retrieval evaluation from generation evaluation?
Suppose the answer is wrong.
There are at least two possibilities:
Retrieval failed
Correct document wasn't retrieved
or:
Retrieval succeeded
Correct document was retrieved
LLM generated a bad answer
Separating the metrics helps identify the actual failure point.
